The multilayer perceptron, or MLP for short, is what most people picture instinctively when they think about a **neural network**.
MLPs have some serious power, and have very real and very useful practical applications we will see later on. Many
concepts are similar to the <LearnLink to="/training/step0/learn/overview">single neuron overview</LearnLink>, so I recommend you brush up on those concepts.

As can be seen in the diagram above, instead of one neuron, we have multiple, arranged in layers. This particular network has
has 2 layers, one **hidden** layer, and an output layer. The input layer is not included in the total layer count. An MLP is sort of a black box, as when trained, the internal layers, weights, 
and biases make it hard for humans to trace the exact logic behind its specific decisions. 

In this section, I will build up more concepts such as batch size, how to optimise resources and compute time, 
some more linear alegebra and calculus, and bring you up to speed with essential machine learning theory.
